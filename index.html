What is the Framework?

The Framework

Key idea
Like I'm a beginner

The accounting framework is the foundation that guides how financial statements are prepared and presented. Think of it as the shared rulebook of ideas that every business and every standard-setter starts from.

Textbook

"The framework sets out the concepts underlying the preparation and presentation of general purpose financial statements prepared by enterprises for a wide range of users." It was issued by the Accounting Standards Board (ASB) of the ICAI in July 2000 and provides the fundamental basis for development of new standards and review of existing standards. It is relevant in the context of the Companies (Accounting Standards) Rules, 2021 and Accounting Standards issued by the ICAI.

Tap a card. The matching person in the 3D town will explain.

Status and scope

Applies to

General-purpose financial statements, usually prepared annually for external users, by all commercial, industrial and business enterprises, in the public or private sector.

Outside scope

Special-purpose reports, for example computations prepared for tax purposes. The framework may still be applied to them to the extent it is not inconsistent with their requirements.

Framework vs Accounting Standards

Framework

The underlying concepts and guiding foundation. Explains why.

Accounting Standards

Specific requirements for particular items. Say exactly what to do.

The Accounting Standard prevails. Nothing in the framework overrides any specific Accounting Standard. In case of conflict, the requirements of the Accounting Standard prevail over those of the framework.

Components of Financial Statements

One year of Meera's shop, told four different ways.

A complete set of financial statements

Like I'm a beginner

Four pieces that describe the same business from different angles: what it owns and owes, what it earned, how cash moved, and the notes that explain the numbers.

Textbook

A complete set of financial statements normally consists of a Balance Sheet, a Statement of Profit and Loss and a Cash Flow Statement together with notes, other statements and explanatory materials that form an integral part of the financial statements.

Why not just one statement?

All components are interrelated because they reflect different aspects of the same transactions. Meera's profit was ₹16,000, yet her cash rose by ₹1,13,000 mostly because of capital and a loan. Profit alone hides the loan; cash alone hides the credit sales. None in isolation can provide all the information a user needs.

Objectives and Users of Financial Statements

Seven people walk up to Meera's shop. Each one wants a different answer from the same statements.

Objective of financial statements

Like I'm a beginner

To give people useful money information so they can make good decisions: Should I invest? Should I lend? Will I be paid?

Textbook

The objective of financial statements is to provide information about the financial position, performance and cash flows of an enterprise that is useful to a wide range of users in making economic decisions.

Chapter 4

Fundamental Accounting Assumptions

Three things every reader assumes you did, unless you tell them otherwise.

The three fundamental assumptions are Going Concern, Accrual and Consistency. Users believe these were followed. So if nothing is written about them, it is assumed they were followed, and no separate disclosure is necessary. If any of them is not followed, that fact must be specifically disclosed.

M

Meera buys new shelves, services her photocopier and orders stock for next year's school season. She plans to stay open. That plan is what "going concern" captures.

Going Concern

Like I'm a beginner

We prepare the accounts believing the shop will keep running. So a shelf is valued as something Meera will keep using, not as something she must sell off tomorrow.

Textbook

Financial statements are normally prepared on the assumption that an enterprise will continue in operation in the foreseeable future and neither there is an intention, nor there is a need to materially curtail the scale of operations. If any financial statement is prepared on a different basis, e.g. when assets are stated at net realisable values, the basis used should be disclosed.

Accrual basis

Like I'm a beginner

Record income when you earn it and costs when you incur them, even if the cash comes or goes later.

Textbook

According to AS 1, revenues and costs are accrued, that is, recognised as they are earned or incurred (and not as money is received or paid) and recorded in the financial statements of the periods to which they relate. Section 128(1) of the Companies Act, 2013 makes it mandatory for companies to maintain accounts on accrual basis only. If any income or expense is recognised on cash basis, that fact should be stated.

Textbook · Example 1

  • Article A purchased on credit in Period 1 for ₹50,000 (paid in Period 2).
  • Article B purchased for cash in Period 1 for ₹2,000.
  • Article A sold for cash in Period 1 for ₹60,000.
  • Article B sold on credit in Period 1 for ₹2,500 (received in Period 2).

Consistency

Like I'm a beginner

Use the same accounting method every year, so this year can be compared fairly with last year.

Textbook

It is assumed that accounting policies are consistent from one period to another. Consistency improves comparability of financial statements through time. An accounting policy can be changed if the change is required (i) by a statute, (ii) by an Accounting Standard, or (iii) for more appropriate presentation of financial statements.

Qualitative Characteristics

Qualitative characteristics

Like I'm a beginner

Four qualities that make accounts worth reading: easy to understand, relevant, reliable and comparable.

Textbook

The qualitative characteristics are attributes that improve the usefulness of information provided in financial statements. Financial statements should observe and maintain Understandability, Relevance, Reliability and Comparability as far as possible within limits of reasonable cost/benefit.

True and Fair View

Before Meera takes her statements to the bank, Fathima the auditor checks them.

True and fair view

Like I'm a beginner

The statements tell the real story of the business, with nothing twisted or hidden, so a reader is not misled.

Textbook

Financial statements are required to show a true and fair view of the performance, financial position and cash flows of an enterprise. The framework does not deal directly with this concept, yet application of the principal qualitative characteristics and appropriate accounting standards normally results in financial statements portraying a true and fair view.

Chapter 8

Recognition of Elements

Seeing something happen is not the same as putting it in the accounts.

Recognition

Like I'm a beginner

To "recognise" an item means to actually write it into the balance sheet or the statement of profit and loss, with a number. Meera may plan to buy a printer, but nothing goes into the books until a real obligation or resource exists and can be measured.

Textbook

An asset must be a resource controlled by the enterprise, probable to generate future economic benefits, with a cost or value that can be measured reliably. A liability is recognised only when outflow of economic resources in settlement of a present obligation can be anticipated and the value of outflow can be reliably measured. A decision by management to acquire assets in the future does not, of itself, give rise to a present obligation; an obligation normally arises only when the asset is delivered or the enterprise enters into an irrevocable agreement.

Chapter 9

Measurement of Elements

At the machinery showroom, one machine can carry four different values.

Measurement

Like I'm a beginner

Measurement means deciding the rupee amount at which an item is shown.

Textbook

Measurement is the process of determining money value at which an element can be recognised in the balance sheet or statement of profit and loss. The framework recognises four alternative measurement bases. All or any of them can be used in varying combinations, subject to the requirements of the Accounting Standards. Accounting Standards largely use historical cost, though for some items other values are permitted, e.g. inventory is valued at year end at the lower of cost and net realisable value.

Chapter 10

Capital, Capital Maintenance and Profit

A full year in the shop: where profit comes from, and how much Meera can safely take home.

Capital introduced

Money the owner puts in. Raises equity, but is not income.

Income

Earned by the business. Raises equity.

Expenses

Incurred by the business. Reduce equity.

Drawings

Taken out by the owner. Reduce equity, but are not an expense.

Live equity and profit equations

CE = OE + C + (I − E) − D
Profit = CE − OE − C + D = (CA − CL) − (OA − OL) − C + D

Capital maintenance

Like I'm a beginner

Before calling money "profit" and taking it home, make sure the shop can still buy back everything it used up. Otherwise the shop slowly shrinks.

Textbook

Capital refers to net assets of a business. It is important for any business to maintain its net assets so as to ensure continued operations at least at the same level year after year. Dividends should not exceed profit after appropriate provisions for replacement of assets consumed in operations. For this reason, the Companies Act does not permit distribution of dividend without providing for depreciation on Property, Plant and Equipment; this may not be enough in case of rising prices. Retained Profit = (CA − CL) − (OA − OL) − C should not be negative.
